At the exhibit

A six-sided craft table seats six people, each with their own kit tray, proverb cards, and a small screen, so nobody waits in line. Blanks, papers, edge rolls, and the word library sit on a turntable in the middle. Tiles stay at the exhibit and are reused.

  1. Choose one of three proverbs and hear it
  2. Make one of its words as a tile
  3. Scan the tile: the screen shows the word, your phone plays it
  4. Finish the proverb with library tiles
  5. Hang the folio on the wall frame (up to 50)
  6. Print a card with your name

Your seat at the table

Everyone gets the same seat setup, so six people can work at once without waiting. The numbers match the steps below.

  1. 1Proverb cards. Three cards with a QR code each. Read them and choose one.
  2. 2Kit tray. A word sticker sheet, a tracing card, a pencil, and a glue stick. Take one blank, one paper, and one edge from the turntable.
  3. 3Seat screen. Hold your tile to it to see the word, how to say it, and what it means. Scan the same code with your phone to hear it.
  4. 4Folio and word library. Set your tile on the folio and take the missing words from the library on the turntable.
  5. 5Wall frame, beside the table. Hang your finished folio.
  6. 6Gift printer, by the frame. Print a card with your name.

Also beside the table: an example board on an easel that shows how a tile is made, the same as the example in step 2.

1Choose a proverb

Every seat has these three proverb cards. Read them, listen, and choose one. You will make one of its words.

2Make your tile

Make one word from your proverb. At the table, blanks, papers, and edges are real objects in the middle of the table. Pick one of each, paste the word, trace it, then add the edge.

4Finish the proverb

Set your tile on the folio, then take the missing words from the word library. At the table, the library is a box of tiles made earlier, and neighbors pass tiles too. Tap a tile on the line to lift it off.

5Hang the folio

A carved wooden frame on the wall with five cords and ten pegs on each, for up to 50 folios. The lowest cord is at a child’s height. Tiles stay at the exhibit: at the end of the day, folios come down and the tiles go back to the library.

Qalam Tiles

Online preview for discussion. Scanning and printing are simulated in this page. The third proverb is a line by the poet al-Mutanabbi (915–965 CE) (source). The wall frame borrows from the Muʿallaqāt, “the suspended” pre-Islamic odes. A later legend says they were written in gold and hung on the Kaaba; most scholars doubt this, and the name may mean “the precious ones” (Britannica). The odes were mostly recited, so every folio can be heard.
